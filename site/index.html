How this was made

Re:Cast was written with the assistance of AI coding tools. The project exists because its author wanted to play this game, with its sound, on a PC, and building an emulator with AI help was the only way they found to do it. It has been tested by playing the game and by an automated test suite, and all the code is open (GPL-3.0) for anyone to read. Some people object to AI use of any type, and that is a fair position: this note is here so you can decide with the facts.

Re:Cast on Android

Play the game the way it was meant to be played

This was a phone game, and now it is one again. The Re:Cast app runs the real game, with its music and sound effects, on an Android phone: the first time it has ever been playable on Android with full sound.

Built for the phone

📱

Flip phone style

A 2005 clamshell drawn around the game, with the V CAST logo, a navigation ring and a real keypad: 5 is Action, * is Magic, 0 is Status. Seven colors and a small bezel, and you can switch styles any time from the game's menu.

🎮

Three touch layouts

A cross (the default), two thumbs, or a direction pad. Every button, each arrow included, can be dragged and resized, and turning the phone sideways gives you bigger buttons.

⚔️

An Action button that reads the game

It watches the game's own action banner and says what it will do: Attack, Open, Talk, Grab, Lift, Throw, or Enter on menus. The softkeys show the game's words (Continue, End), and you can tap the game's own menus.

🎵

Full sound, decoded on the phone

The tuned music synth and the phone's compressed voice-codec sound effects, decoded with Android's own codec, in step with the action. The Wonderland theme plays on the Wonderland screen.

🎚️

A sound screen

Play every tune, pick a ready-made mix or save your own, and star your favorites. Have the game play your own recordings (FLAC, MP3 and more) instead of the synth, matched to its volume.

🔠

Sharp text, the game's font

The game's tiny text is redrawn sharply at the phone's resolution, and the app's menus and buttons use the Kingdom Hearts menu font.

💾

Saves that travel

Save states, quick save and load, and autosaves, all from the game's menu. Export a save state to carry on from the same spot on your PC, or bring one over from it (the same game files and settings are needed).

🏆

The shared leaderboard

Post your scores to the same anonymous board as the PC version. The app asks first, and you can switch it off.

Re:Cast for Android is coming soon: one zip with the app and the game files, for Android 8 or newer on a 64-bit phone. Want it sooner, or have a phone you could test it on? Say so in Jiminy's Journal.

About

In 2005, Superscape and Disney Mobile made a full 3D Kingdom Hearts for Verizon's V CAST phones: Sora, the Keyblade, Maleficent's Castle, all on a phone with a 176×220 screen. The phones, the service and the store are long gone.

Re:Cast runs the game's own code, unchanged, on an emulated phone processor and recreates the phone features it relies on. It draws the real 3D, plays the music and sound effects, and carries you from world to world through the game's own chapter flow.

Playable on PC with full sound for the first time, and coming soon to Android.

Until now this game ran only on its original phones, on a collector's modified Zeebo console, or without any audio in an Android emulator. As far as we know, Re:Cast is the first emulator to play it with its music and sound effects in context: the game's real soundtrack and effects, in step with the action.

A missing chapter. The second episode, Wonderland, has never been recovered. Re:Cast bridges it: after the Island you get the game's surviving Wonderland pages, then carry on to Agrabah. Help find it.

Features

Authentic

⚙️

The game's own code

Re:Cast runs the real game program and Superscape's 3D engine, unchanged, on an emulated phone processor. It is the game itself, not a remake.

Quality of life

💾

Save states

Snapshot anywhere with F5 and load with F9: nine slots, automatic autosaves, and backups of the game's own saves.

▶️

A launcher that remembers

A Continue button with a preview of where you left off, one-click setup, and your settings kept.

🎨

Picture filters

Crisp pixels, smooth, sharp pixels or Scale2x. Press F11 to compare them live.

🔠

Smooth hi-res text

The game's tiny 176×220 text is redrawn sharply at your window's resolution, so menus and dialogue stay crisp at any size.

🏆

A shared leaderboard

The original ranking server is long gone, so Re:Cast brings it back. Scores are shared anonymously (only the world, score, Munny, EXP, Level and time, plus the kind of device and the Re:Cast version, never who you are) so you can see how you rank. You can switch sharing off. A run that used a save state, or that doesn't beat a score you already shared, is not shared.

⚡

Faster 3D, same picture

Speed-ups for the game's 3D engine, checked pixel for pixel against the original. On by default; each one can be switched off in the Options tab, with an info bubble explaining what it does.

🎮

Set your own keys

Move, action, magic, status, the two softkeys and Backspace can each be changed to whatever you like on the launcher's Controls tab. A key already in use tells you so.

📦

Everything included

One installer for Windows, Mac or Linux, with its own Python, ffmpeg and game files. No setup, no extra downloads.

Screenshots

The launcher

Re:Cast opens with a launcher that picks up where you left off, keeps your save states, and lets you set up the picture and the music before you play. It finds the game files itself the first time.

The music

The game’s music was written by composer Ian Livingstone in 2004, for a game they never played. Years later the YouTuber 13th Vessel got in touch with them, and posted the complete original soundtrack: “KINGDOM HEARTS V-Cast [Complete Original Soundtrack]”.

The video’s description holds an interview with the composer, in which they answer questions about the music. It is an interesting read if you like this soundtrack.

Download the soundtrack

The game’s music is available as audio files from KHInsider: Kingdom Hearts V CAST (Mobile) (gamerip) (2005).

If you prefer those recordings to Re:Cast’s built-in synthesizer, the game can play them instead, tune by tune. In the launcher’s Sound tab, pick the tune, Choose... the matching file, press Match to even out the volume, then tick Use the recording in the game. Re:Cast fits each recording to the game’s own length for that tune, so the game plays exactly as before; untick it to go back to the built-in music.

The Sound tab also has sliders and ready-made mixes for the built-in synth, and an Advanced... window to play every tune through a General MIDI SoundFont instead, with the free fluidsynth program (not included). The built-in synth renders each tune once and keeps it so it plays instantly after that; Clear cached music in that same window gives the space back if you want it.

Where are my saves?

In a folder called .khvcemu in your home folder, kept safe across updates and uninstalls. Save states also appear in the launcher with a preview picture.

Controls

These are the keys as they come. W, A, S, D, Space (the action key), F, Z, Q, E and Backspace (back: once pauses, twice goes to the title screen) can be changed on the launcher's Controls tab.

It runs slowly on my computer

Re:Cast is emulating a whole phone, and the 3D engine is demanding. On the PC it was built on, the game runs at about 25 frames per second in most scenes and about 19 in the busiest (a fight, Agrabah's lava cave). Slower machines show fewer frames rather than slowing the game down. The speed enhancements and the steady frame rate (launcher, Options) are on by default.

Screenshots, and freeing up space

F12 takes a screenshot (a small message confirms where it was saved). The launcher's Options tab lists them with a preview of the one you've selected: icons let you choose the folder, open the selected picture, open the folder, or delete it. On Windows a deleted screenshot goes to the Recycle Bin, after a question you can turn off and bring back with Restore default settings.

The built-in music synth renders each tune once and keeps it so it plays at once next time; it takes a few MB. The Sound tab's Advanced... window has Clear cached music if you want that space back (the game renders the tunes again when it needs them).
